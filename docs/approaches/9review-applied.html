9 Review of applied methods

Alternative labels: methodological studies;1; 2 meta-research;3 research-on-research; systematic surveys; scoping review; among many more terms that appear in practice4 with little consensus5

Definition and typical features

Reviews of applied methods examine how researchers implement methods in health research or which challenges they encounter. They sample and systematically analyze studies or other applications of methods such as in guideline panels. Sources of such applications include published journal articles, trial registries, preprints, or other sources. Sampling strategies are important and may be random to assess prevalence, purposeful to focus on high-impact studies, stratified to ensure representation of subgroups, matched such as pairs of protocols and corresponding publications, or complete. Reviews of applied methods may be nested within systematic review of health research. Typical outcomes are items of quality such as appropriate use of a method or transparent reporting. The analysis is usually based on simple quantitative measures, such as prevalence. Researchers often extract data using pre-specified forms, sometimes based on pre-existing frameworks (such as reporting guidelines) or use qualitative methods to derive categories.

  • A strength is that these studies reflect realistic practice, including human error.
  • Challenges arise from sampling and reporting issues.
