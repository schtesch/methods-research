11 Scoping review of methods research

Alternative labels:

Definition and typical features

Scoping reviews include a broad research question and a systematic search, and aim to provide an overview of existing methods research on a methodological topic or available methods discussed in such methods research. Scoping reviews may present simple descriptive statistics (absolute and relative frequencies), often based on a qualitative categorisation or mapping against some pre-existing framework. They do not typically include a quality assessment or synthesis of study results.

  • Strengths include transparency and comprehensiveness.
  • A challenge is the difficult search process.

Typical purposes and corresponding examples

Identify available methods: Identify definitions of intention-to-treat;1 Review of causal methods to allow predictions under hypothetical interventions;2 identify criteria for assessing the credibility of subgroup analyses;3 identify factors influencing the trustworthiness of non-randomized studies;4 identify methods addressing bias in adaptive trial designs.5;

Identify available methods research on a specific topic: Identify publications that suggest a process for developing methods guidance.6 identify and map guidance for evidence synthesis 7

Develop guidance for understanding and applying methods: inform guidance on integrating qualitative methods in randomized trials;8 scoping review informed guidance for developing core outcome sets.9

Develop reporting guidelines: scoping review informed the development of a new reporting guideline for factorial trials.10

Develop quality assessment instruments: scoping review of previous instruments informed the development of a new instrument to assess the quality of systematic reviews.11
