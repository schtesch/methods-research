15 Informal approaches

Terminology: narrative approaches

Definition and features

In informal approaches, researchers propose, explain, discuss, or recommend methods without a formal methods research design. They may selectively cite methodological publications, rely on professional authority and persuasive narrative, integrate expert opinions without structured collaboration or formal consensus, or use theory, examples, or simulations to illustrate points rather than to answer a methods research question.

  • Strengths include the flexibility to present methodological ideas efficiently.
  • A limitation is the reliance on selective evidence and author judgment.

Typical objectives and corresponding examples

Examples exist for most objectives, including:

Develop guidance for understanding and applying methods: preventing common misunderstandings of randomisation;1 sample size calculation in cluster trials;2 explanation why guidelines should seldom make good practice statements.3

Propose a new method: proposal for a random invitation single arm design.4

Summarize the results of methods research: overview of approaches for evaluating heterogeneous treatment effects.5

Develop software and code: web application for Bayes Factor Design Analysis;6 netmeta, an R package for network meta-analysis.7
