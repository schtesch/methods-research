About this initiative

Welcome to the websites for the new Typology of Methods Research (TYPE-ME) - a living resource for methods researchers who work in the context of health research and psychology. The typology is the product of an expert-panel process with empirical testing.

The typology

Start with the condensed version for quick orientation, or browse the full typology using the side bar or the interactive overview figure.

TipWhy do we need TYPE-ME?

The field of methods research includes many study types, but their definitions, terminology, and boundaries remain unclear.

This lack of clarity

  • complicates planning and reporting,
  • creates uncertainty about labels,
  • limits searchability and indexing in research databases,
  • reduces the visibility of methods research as a field,
  • obscures what constitutes methodological evidence, and
  • hinders standard setting.

TYPE-ME brings these types together as one family and clarifies their similarities, differences, strengths, and difficult boundaries.

TipWhat is methods research?

Methods research aims to develop, evaluate, refine, or provide guidance on research methods, support their implementation or de-implementation, or synthesize existing methods research.

Methods research addresses methods across the whole research lifecycle: planning, conducting, analyzing and interpreting, reporting, summarizing, evaluating, and implementing research.

Methods research also addresses challenges that require methodological solutions and the consequences of methodological choices.

TYPE-ME focuses on research methods used in health research.

TipHow does TYPE-ME organize methods research?

TYPE-ME organizes methods research along two cross-cutting dimensions:

  • Approach: How do researchers conduct the methods research?
  • Objective: Why do researchers conduct the methods research?

The detailed typology includes 15 approach types and 14 objective types. One approach can serve several objectives, and one objective can draw on several approaches (see overview figure).

You can characterize a methodological study by pairing approach and objective, much as you would characterize any study by its approach and objective. Three examples:

  • A researcher re-analyzes several published datasets to see which of two methods gives more precise estimates: an experimental analysis of real datasets (A3) to compare methods (O8).
  • A researcher runs a Delphi study to identify key items for a new reporting checklist: a consensus study (A8) to develop a reporting guideline (O10).
  • A researcher interviews trialists about why they do not use core outcome sets: a qualitative study (A7) to research the dissemination and implementation of methods (O13).

For each type, we provide a definition, possible labels, strengths and limitations, examples, and boundary notes where needed.

TipWhat can I use TYPE-ME for?

TYPE-ME can help you:

  • plan methods research by clarifying the study objectives and possible design choices;
  • report methods research by supporting clearer labels, definitions, and design descriptions;
  • classify methods research when reviewing a methodological topic;
  • inform indexing and searching by supporting article types, publication types, index terms, and search filters;
  • define inclusion criteria for methodological journals, databases, funding calls, or conferences;
  • design programs of methods research by showing how different types can build on each other, for example from method development to evaluation, guidance development, and implementation;
  • support teaching by presenting methods research as a structured field with connected types; and
  • identify guidance needs by showing which types of methods research lack specific guidance for conduct, reporting, or quality assessment.

Treat the types as archetypes, not as mutually exclusive boxes. A study may combine several approaches or fall between related types. For broad planning, teaching, discussion, or preliminary classification, you may want to start with the condensed version, which groups the main types into a single list of 10.

TipHow did we develop TYPE-ME?

An international team of 11 methodologists developed TYPE-ME through repeated discussion, written feedback, and testing. The team members were:

  • Stefan Schandelmaier
  • Malena Chiaborelli
  • Julian Hirt
  • Anne-Laure Boulesteix
  • Matthias Briel
  • Livia Puljak
  • Zachary Munn
  • Hanan Khalil
  • Dawid Pieper
  • Georg Heinze
  • Richard Hooper

In two testing rounds, team members independently classified 52 methods research articles from 26 journals, contributing 494 individual article assessments. Testing helped us refine definitions and labels, clarify difficult boundaries, and document remaining disagreements.

TYPE-ME is the product of an expert-panel process with empirical testing and transparent documentation of remaining disagreements, rather than a set of consensus-approved statements.

We will continue to refine TYPE-ME through application, user feedback, and further testing.
